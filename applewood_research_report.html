Applewood Plumbing Heating & Electric: Research Report

The business and what it does

Applewood Plumbing Heating & Electric is a plumbing, heating, and electrical contractor based in Denver, Colorado. The company has been in business for 53 years, since 1973. Its BBB profile lists Mr. Josh Ward under Business Management.

Contact and location details:

Website: https://applewoodfixit.com

Email: custservice@applewoodfixit.com

Phone: (720) 249-5122 / (303) 328-3060

Address: 5000 W 29th Ave, Denver, CO 80212

The business is a large, established multi-trade shop, not a one-person operation. It is BBB Accredited since 1992 and holds an A+ rating, with 341 BBB reviews. It has 26 BBB complaints in the last 3 years and 5 in the last 12 months. Source: BBB profile

The issue

Customer feedback shows a breakdown in communication around appointments: no callback after repeated outreach, and late cancellations with no useful alternative offered. The work itself is not the complaint. The communication is.

A BBB complaint from 2026 describes repeated outreach with no callback, on a water heater warranty matter:

"I have reached out to the company several times and no one has gotten back to me recently. They just keep saying that someone will contact me."

A BBB complaint from 2026 describes a cancelled air conditioning appointment:

"Our air conditioning went out on Friday. We called and set up an appointment for Sunday, the soonest they could get us in. The window was from 3 - 5, THEY CALLED AT 3:58 to let us know they COULD NOT MAKE IT and the soonest they could get us in is TUESDAY!"

The complaints are about communication around appointments, not about the workmanship. Nothing in the sources reviewed here faults the work. The A+ rating and 53 years in business are the proof the work is good. The issue is that a customer who reaches out repeatedly hears nothing back, and a customer with a broken air conditioner gets a cancellation call at 3:58 for a 3 to 5 window, with the next opening two days out.

Why it matters

A multi-trade shop books far more appointments than a one-person shop, so a dropped confirmation or a late cancellation hits more customers. The A+ rating and 53 years prove the work is good, which makes the communication gap the whole story: a customer who cannot get a callback, and a customer who waits all weekend for a Sunday window only to get a cancellation call at 3:58, are not complaining about the repair. They are complaining about being left without an answer.

The 3:58 cancellation call is the sharpest single detail. A customer with no air conditioning set aside a Sunday afternoon, and learned two minutes before the end of the window that no one was coming, with the next opening two days out. A text at the moment the schedule slipped, with the next window already in it, would have changed that afternoon completely. With 5 complaints in the last 12 months and 26 in 3 years, the pattern is easy to prove and it is public.

The proposed solution

The proposed solution is an appointment confirmation, late and cancellation SMS system with a missed-call auto-reply and a missed-call log, designed to close the communication gap around appointments. It is the same system as Denver Ducts, adapted to a larger multi-trade shop.

What the system does:

Confirms the appointment window by SMS when a customer books.

Texts the customer right away if the crew runs late or the appointment is cancelled, with the next available window already in the message, instead of a last-minute call.

Replies automatically via SMS to any call the office misses, and writes the event to a missed-call log, so a request for a callback can never fall out of the process unnoticed.

Gives the office clear visibility into every missed call and tracks whether it was followed up.

What the system does not do:

Does not overhaul the current dispatch process.

Does not replace the existing CRM.

Does not change pricing or scheduling policy.

Does not change the workmanship or the field work.
