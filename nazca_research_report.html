Nazca Lawn Services: Research Report

The business and what it does

Nazca Lawn Services is a locally owned and family-operated landscaping company based in the Denver metropolitan area. In business since 2005, 21 years, the company is owned by Jhonny Suarez. The company manages recurring and seasonal lawn care, including weekly mowing, sprinkler installation, sprinkler repairs, and winterization blowouts, plus mulch, aeration, power raking, and fertilization. They also complete project-based work such as sod installation, fence building, retaining walls, paver patios, tree and shrub planting, yard cleanup, and junk removal.

The business operates Monday through Friday from 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM, and Saturday from 8:00 AM to 4:00 PM. They maintain three listed addresses: a primary site listing at 1704 W Baltic Pl in Englewood, a Better Business Bureau headquarters address at 879 S Osage St in Denver, and a PO Box in Arvada. Customers contact the company via email at jhonny@nazcaservices.com or by phone at (303) 910-2427 and (720) 338-9816.

Customer satisfaction regarding the actual landscaping work is high. The company holds a 4.8-star average across 72 reviews on BestProsInTown, a 4.5-star average on 8 Angi reviews, and a 4.0-star average on 12 Google Business reviews. The Better Business Bureau lists them with an A+ rating and zero complaints in the last three years, though the business is not BBB accredited.

The issue

The primary operational gap is inconsistent scheduling follow-through and communication. A documented pattern exists across multiple years of missed appointments where the customer receives no advance notice. The owner is the scheduler, so when he forgets, there is no backup.

In a June 2022 review on BestProsInTown, customer L C. detailed a missed appointment: "In late spring of 2022, I scheduled with this company. Having used them in the past, we felt comfortable scheduling them for yard work again. This time, however, he never showed up. I spoke to him twice to confirm..."

An undated review on usa-services.worldorgs.com highlights a similar experience regarding a sprinkler activation: "This is a horrible company. And by company I mean one man named Johnny. He was supposed to come to turn my sprinkler system on one day. I woke up at 3pm because he said he would be there between 3-5. I work nightshift so this was super early for me. It was 6pm and he didnt show up. I called and he said he could come because it was hailing? Well it wasnt even raining and it would have been nice to get a call to cancel. We rescheduled for a week later at the same time. Again I woke up early even though I worked the three nights before. 3 hours went by and he still didnt show up. He said he spaced on it."

Another undated review on the same site notes the owner failed to show up to mow a lawn after the customer returned from a trip: "Jhonny mowed my lawn for two years. They did an okay job for a great price. All of a sudden I was out of town for a few weeks and came back to a 1ft of grass. I called jhonny he told me that he was busy with his regular customers. I thought I was a regular customer I told him? He said hed get there as soon as he could. He never showed. Bye jhonny."

A fourth undated review on the same site describes a late arrival with a text notification: "Was an hour late after giving us a two hour window (they did text us to let us know) and then did a horrible job installing sod as a patch job."

It is important to distinguish between the quality of the work and the reliability of the schedule. The reviews consistently praise the lawn care itself. The problem is the follow-through. One review, the sod installation case, shows that communication sometimes happens. The gap is inconsistency. It depends on the owner remembering to send the message.

Recent reviews indicate the scheduling problem is not a daily failure right now, but rather a recurring historical vulnerability tied to how appointments are tracked. In October 2026, a customer wrote: "I've used Nazca for lawn mowing for years and they are always super reliable, on-time, efficient and reasonably priced. I will continue to use them in the future." In June 2026, another customer wrote: "Jhonny and his team do good, reliable work...responsive with thoughtful problem solving and always professional." A May 2026 review stated: "We used Nazca Lawn Services when we were in Denver. They did a fantastic job maintaining our lawn, garden beds, and sprinklers... Jhonny is always responsive and helpful."

Why it matters

A lawn care business lives on the weekly visit happening on schedule and the seasonal job being confirmed. If a mowing appointment is missed, the grass keeps growing. If a seasonal job like a sprinkler blowout is missed, the job is not confirmed and the customer is left waiting at home for a visit that does not happen.

The issue is not the work itself. A customer who waits for hours, or returns home to an uncut lawn after being away for weeks, loses trust in the schedule. For a weekly-mow business, a forgotten appointment is a lost regular. When a customer cannot rely on the visit happening, they are more likely to seek a different provider. The owner is the scheduler, so when he forgets, there is no backup, and the customer gets no call when the appointment does not happen.

The proposed solution

The proposed solution introduces an automated SMS scheduling tool paired with a simple daily job board and missed-call recovery. The goal is to remove the reliance on one person's memory for customer communication.

What it does: When a customer books a mow, cleanup, or sprinkler job, the system sends an SMS text confirming the appointment window. If the crew runs late, is rescheduled, or the window is missed, the system texts the customer automatically. The customer is not left waiting in silence. A simple job board displays the day's visits, so nothing gets "spaced." If a call is missed, the system sends an automatic SMS reply and writes the call to a simple log, so a missed appointment or callback request can never fall out of the process unnoticed again.

What it does not do: This system does not overhaul the current field dispatch process. It does not replace any customer relationship management software. It does not change pricing. It does not change the lawn care work itself. It is a narrow fix for a specific problem: scheduling communication and visibility.
