L&G Cleaning Services: Research Report

1. Executive Summary

The Business: L&G Cleaning Services is a family-owned cleaning company established in 2011, serving the Aurora and greater Denver, Colorado area. Reviews praise quality, reliability, and communication. The Issue: The owner, Luis Guzman, reviews, prices, and confirms every quote and appointment request himself. This creates a bottleneck, acting as a ceiling on the company's growth and consuming the owner’s time. The Proposed Product: A custom automated intake, quoting, and scheduling FastAPI microservice. This solution will compute firm quotes using the company’s published pricing rules, check real crew availability, and issue automatic SMS/email confirmations for standard cleanings, routing the complex edge cases to the owner for manual approval.

3. The Issue: The Owner Bottleneck

Despite having the necessary structured data to automate bookings, every scheduling decision funnels through one person. The website's own copy repeats this bottleneck:

  • Homepage hero: "Ask a question or request a free estimate. Luis confirms pricing and appointments."
  • "How It Works" step 2: "Request a day and arrival window, Mon–Sat. Luis confirms availability and your appointment."
  • Quote form ending: "This is a quote request, not a confirmed appointment."
  • AI Assistant (Maggie): "Share the details of the cleaning you need. The Advisor organizes the request for Luis to review and confirm your final quote."
  • Service-specific interventions:
  • Deep cleaning: "Luis reviews your priorities"
  • Move-in/out: "Luis can confirm what is included"
  • Free return cleaning: "Luis confirms scheduling"
  • Damage reports: "Luis reviews the situation"
  • Modifications: Cancellations and rescheduling require a manual call or email with no self-serve option. Last-minute/same-day bookings state: "Call (303) 944-4318 to check availability".
  • Site-wide footer: "Monday–Saturday · Appointments confirmed by Luis."

In mechanics, the AI and intake forms collect data; a submitted request yields nothing until Luis replies.

4. Why It Matters

  • Single Point of Failure: Luis is the sole bottleneck for pricing, availability, confirmation, changes, and rescheduling. Every request waits on his manual reply, including evenings and weekends when he is working on jobs.
  • Strict Growth Ceiling: This manual intervention caps how many bookings the business can take in and limits how fast a customer receives an answer.
  • Wasted Automation Potential: The inputs required to automate these decisions are being collected in a structured format. The current quote form captures service type, bedrooms, bathrooms, square footage, frequency, add-ons (fridge/oven, interior windows, laundry & ironing), preferred dates, and specific arrival windows (8–10am, 10am–12pm, 12–2pm, 2–4pm). The pricing logic and discount rules are standardized and published. The data is prepared for automation; the final decision remains manual.
  • Important Distinction: This is an owner-time and growth-ceiling problem, not a reputation or customer service problem. Existing reviews praise the business as "easy to schedule."

5. The Product Idea: Automated Intake, Quoting & Scheduling Service

To free the owner's time and remove the ceiling on growth, we propose a custom middleware solution that transitions standard bookings to a zero-touch flow, keeping Luis in the loop for complex edge cases.

  • End-to-End Customer Flow: The customer submits the existing structured form. Instead of resting in an inbox, the service computes a firm quote using the published pricing ranges and discount rules. It then checks real-time crew availability, secures the calendar block, and sends SMS and email confirmations and reminders to the client.
  • Luis Override: Standard recurring and one-off house cleans are automated. Complex edge cases (deep cleans, move-ins/move-outs, and post-construction) go to a manual approval queue for Luis to review before confirmation.
  • Technical Shape:
  • Core: A lightweight FastAPI service acting as the central service.
  • Quote Engine: A rules-based computational engine that translates the site's published ranges and discount matrices into exact quotes.
  • Availability Module: An integration that checks the business's existing schedule to map available 2-hour arrival windows against crew capacity. [CLANCY AUDIT: this assumes a digital schedule exists. What tool Luis uses today is unknown (see §7). If bookings are tracked on paper or by memory, v1 includes standing up a shared calendar first, before the integration.]
  • Communications: Automated SMS and email dispatch for instant confirmations and pre-appointment reminders.
  • Operational Handoff: Delivery includes a complete Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) documenting how to manage the new flow.

6. Scope for a First Version (v1)

In Scope for v1:

  • The FastAPI middleware service linking the intake form to the quote engine.
  • The rules-based pricing calculator for standard and recurring home/apartment cleans.
  • Basic calendar/availability check integration.
  • Automated SMS and email confirmations/reminders for approved bookings.
  • Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) documentation.

Out of Scope for v1:

  • Automated approvals for complex services (deep cleans, move-in/out, post-construction, commercial). These will be routed to the Luis Override step.
  • A custom-built CRM or calendar application (the service will integrate with the schedule tooling Luis uses).

Delivery Outline (35/40/25 Milestone Shape):

  • Milestone 1 (35%): Kickoff & Rules Lock: Finalizing the exact mathematical logic for the pricing engine and confirming the calendar integration constraints.
  • Milestone 2 (40%): Working Demo: Delivery of a functional end-to-end pipeline (intake form → automated quote → calendar block → test email/SMS notifications).
  • Milestone 3 (25%): Final Delivery: Production deployment of the FastAPI service and handover of the documented SOP.

7. Risks, Limits & Unknowns

Before initiating Milestone 1, the following operational unknowns must be confirmed with Luis:

  • What specific tool (if any) tracks crew schedules and bookings today (e.g., Google Calendar, a specific CRM, a spreadsheet, or paper)?
  • How long does a manual quote reply take today?
  • What is the current weekly volume of inbound requests?
  • Does the existing AI "Advisor" (Maggie) deliver its intake summaries via email, or do they live in a proprietary dashboard?
