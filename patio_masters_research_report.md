## The issue

Customer feedback shows a breakdown in communication on live projects: weeks of silence between milestones, no status updates, and callback requests that go unanswered. Customers report no response to repeated requests to speak to the owner, a project 15 weeks overdue, a promised refund with no paperwork, and calls answered only from an unfamiliar number.

A BBB complaint describes a project 15 weeks overdue:

"Communication has been unacceptable and we have on multiple occasions requested to talk to the owner with no response."

A BBB complaint from 2026 describes a 31 August meeting with the owner and a promised refund. This is the customer's report of what the owner agreed, not a verified fact about the refund itself. The company has not confirmed the agreement in any source reviewed here, and the refund has not been verified as paid.

"On 31 August, the owner of Denver Patio met with me at my home. He explicitly agreed to cancel the contract and issue a full refund of my deposit... Since that meeting, weeks have passed with no updates, no paperwork, and no refund. The company is actively ignoring my phone calls. When I attempt to call from my primary number, there is no answer. However, when I called from an alternative number, a representative answered immediately, so they are intentionally screening my calls."

A BBB review from Samantha B. sums up the pattern:

"They can't wait to take your money, but they will take their time on returning your phone calls or providing ANY kind of update."

The complaints are about communication on live projects, not about the finished patio work. Nothing in the sources reviewed here faults the build quality. The issue is status updates, callback follow-through, and responsiveness after the deposit is paid.

## Why it matters

A project-based business takes a deposit up front and then works for weeks. The customer has already paid, and the only thing holding the relationship together between milestones is communication. When that goes silent, the customer has no way to know whether the job is on track, delayed, or stalled. The call-screening complaint is the sharpest single detail: a customer who can only reach the company by calling from a number it does not recognize.

The BBB record makes the cost visible. An F rating with 4 complaints in the last 12 months is public, and it is the first thing a prospective customer sees when they check the company. Patio work is high-ticket, so one lost referral and one public F rating cost real money. The deposit is paid up front and then the customer hears nothing for weeks, which makes the silence worse.

## The proposed solution

The proposed solution is a project-status SMS system with a missed-call auto-reply and a callback-request log, designed to close the communication gap on live projects.

What the system does:

Sends a status text to the customer at each project milestone, so the customer is never left guessing where the job stands.

Replies automatically via SMS to any call the office misses, and writes the event to a callback-request log, so a request to speak to the owner can never fall out of the process unnoticed.

Gives the office clear visibility into every missed call and tracks whether it was followed up.

What the system does not do:

Does not overhaul the current project-management process.

Does not replace the existing CRM.

Does not change pricing or contract structures.

Does not change the build quality or the finished work.
